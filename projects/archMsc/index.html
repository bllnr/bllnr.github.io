Revisiting the Picturesque

The Preschool Building as Garden

MSc Thesis in Architecture and Urban Design
Chalmers School of Architecture | Year: 22
Supervisor & Examiner: Daniel Norell

Project Idea

Research suggests that natural environments support children's development by providing a dynamic setting that engages them emotionally and stimulates imagination and movement. While outdoor nature preschools are increasingly common, their indoor facilities remain conventional.

What practices of the 18th century landscape garden are relevant to architecture?

How can these practices be adapted to architecture?

How can these practices inform the design of a preschool?

Part 1: Translating the 18th-Century Landscape

By studying historical landscape gardening texts (such as Thomas Whately’s 1770 Observations on Modern Gardening), this thesis identifies key qualities—asymmetry, irregular shapes, partially concealed views, and oblique movement.

The Delayed View

Partial concealment, layered interior apertures, and visual depths that provoke curiosity and a desire to explore.


"The eye must not dart down the whole length immediately, but should be conducted towards the point with some circuity and delay (Whately, 1770)."


1. The eye travels over a multitude of objects. 2. The view is partially concealed. 3. An object may be seen from several vantage points.

Oblique Movement

Diverging paths of physical travel from direct geometric sightlines, ensuring a changeful and unfolding layout sequence.


"When a building, or other object, has been once viewed from its proper point, the foot should never travel to it by the same path, which the eye has travelled before. Lose the object, and draw nigh, obliquely (Shenstone, 1764)."


1. Various paths are available. 2. Movement happens through sequences of spaces. 3. Movement is oblique to the view and the final destination.

Emotional Character

Alternating emotional zones using contrasting architectural spatial shifts: transition from dark to light, narrow to broad, and high to low volumes.


"Gardening indeed possesses one advantage, never to be equalled in the other art: in various scenes, it can raise successively all the different emotions above-mentioned. But to produce that delicious effect, the garden must be extensive, so as to admit a slow succession; for a small garden, comprehended at one view, ought to be confined to one expression it may be gay, it may be sweet, it maybe gloomy: but an attempt to mix these would create a jumble (Kames, 1830)."


1. Movement may happen between spaces of contrasting qualities. 2. Movement may happen through a succession of intensification of a quality (e.g. small->larger->big). 3. Curiosity and playfulness may be stimulated by movement from rectilinear to freeform spaces.

Part 2: Building Blocks of Picturesque Space

What we see while we inhabit, how we move in, how we experience architectural space—this depends not only on the characteristics and structure of the space itself, but also on how the space relates to other spaces. To the end of creating Picturesque Spaces, i.e. spaces that have the qualities of partial and delayed views oblique movement, that use character and have variety, I needed spatial tools that would help in creating such spaces. The Drama of Space (Kleine, 2017) focuses on the experience of space as dependent on the organization and relation between spaces, through the lens of spatial dramaturgy, and equipped me with suitable tools. Here I also began to explore potential ways to use the tactics in the context of a design project that interprets the landscape garden.

Configuring Spaces

In the context of landscape gardening; the relation between adjacent spaces decides what we can see from a vantage point (a full, direct view or a partial, delayed view), how we move in and between spaces (moving straight or obliquely), and to what degree the character of a space is affected by adjacent spaces.

Interleaving Spaces

I see interleaving space as a particularly promising way to configure Picturesque Space. Interleaving spaces have a quality of duality between enclosing and opening, and depending on how the comprising spaces are combined, there are almost endless possibilities of directing view and movement. Additionally, interleaving spaces allows for creating many layers of spaces, making it a rich tool for framing views. Interleaving space allows for creating views that are partial and delayed, particularly through creating complex inward views and through views.

Framing Views

Inward views, outward views and through views are the most relevant types of view to our discussion of the landscape garden, allowing us views that are partial and delayed. Specifically, it is possible to imagine through views that are comprised of many layers, framing the view in a complex way, allowing a partial view of both the spaces in between and the final view.

Part 3: Project Application & Site Strategy

Site: Fjällbo Ängar, North East Gothenburg

The site chosen is a plot south of the nature area Fjällbo Ängar, located in the North East of Gothenburg. The south part of the area (close to the plot) is characterized by hilly meadows, growing into rocky cliffs further north. Furthest north in the area is the lake Bergsjön. The lake has outlets into small rills, one of which runs out onto the plot where the project is placed.


Topography

Elevation from 26-30 m over ocean level. The slope has its aspect to the south-south east.


Biological context

Grassy field on the middle of the site. Messy thicket of trees east of the brook. Mountain north of the site. Mature trees are found on the outer parts of the plot, many on the eastern part, and a few in the middle of the plot. One large rock on the north part of the site. Some smaller rocks close to the bridge over the brook. The bridge is without handrails, made from large blocks of stone. The brook makes pleasant noises, with the water falling from a low height in some parts.


Transportation & Utilities

Opportunity for this on the southern part of the plot.


Visibility

View to the mountain in the north, the park north of the plot with gently sloping hills and clumps of trees in foreground. Hills can be seen in the far south.

Program

The program chosen to apply the interpretation of picturesque space to is a pre-school, with two groups of 15-20 children each, divided by age. The basic program for each unit is a home room, meant for dining and gathering the group, play rooms, a room for naps and story time. The two units share a music & drama room and an atelier. The program also has supporting rooms for staff and the functioning of the building.

Part 4: The Spatial Journey

SEQUENCE A

The Foyer & Gathering Space

Immediate exposure to changeful internal through-views. Scaled down micro-architectures like play lofts, children's tunnels, and soft curving parameters are nestled directly into structural zones.

Space for play and gathering. Contains both open spaces as well as small niches. Outward views and through views are employed.

SEQUENCE B

From Active Playroom to the Nap & Story Room

A transition of emotional spatial character. Moving from an active, open rectangular play space, users pass into a circular, enclosed subterranean-like resting hub complete with an overhead lighting oculus.

Domed circular story & nap room, partly subterranean.
